## Likely questions (short answers)

- **"If the LP is exact, why do Dinic and Hopcroft–Karp exist?"** They exploit the graph structure directly and are faster. The LP is the general tool: one solver, no problem-specific algorithm. E4 measures the gap.
- **"Why is the matching LP not exact on a triangle?"** x_e = ½ on all three edges is feasible with value 1.5, but a matching has at most 1 edge. The triangle's matrix is not TU. Fixing this needs Edmonds' blossom constraints (out of scope).
- **"What does 'no branching needed' mean?"** Branch-and-bound first solves the LP relaxation. With a TU matrix that LP is already integral, so the solver stops at the root node.
- **"Are overnight shifts NP-hard then?"** No. The matrix has circular 1s and is not TU, but it can still be solved with a few LP solves (Bartholdi, Orlin & Ratliff 1980, unverified). Beyond our scope; the point is that TU itself is lost.
- **"Where is LP duality used?"** The dual of the bipartite matching LP is the vertex cover LP. Both are TU, so both have integral optima, and strong duality makes the optimal values equal: König's theorem.
