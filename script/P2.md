## Slide 1 · Exhaustive tests · approximately 45 seconds

We know why TU matters. How do we recognise it?

Brute force checks the determinant of every square submatrix. All must give minus one, zero or one. The triangle fails with determinant two. For size k, choose k rows and k columns: the formula counts these choices. This is exponential in the worst case.

Ghouila-Houri instead requires every row subset to admit plus or minus signs, keeping each column sum within minus one, zero and one. Alternating signs on these four-cycle rows gives zero. That's one subset; all must pass. Direct enumeration is still exponential.

## Slide 2 · Structural recognition and handoff · approximately 45 seconds

Seymour's approach recursively decomposes each piece. These sketches show the connections: a one-sum has none; a two-sum has one rank-one connector; this three-sum form has two. An outer product, a b transpose, has rank one when both vectors are nonzero.

At the leaves, we recognise network matrices, their transposes, or exceptional pieces. If all children are TU and the sum conditions hold, their parent is TU. Repeat this upwards to certify the original matrix.

Recognition takes polynomial time. We will use CMR. Feilin will now discuss how we solve the LP once we know the matrix is TU.
